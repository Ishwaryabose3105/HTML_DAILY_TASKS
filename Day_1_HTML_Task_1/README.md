A.P.J. Abdul Kalam's History

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam (15 October 1931 – 27 July 2015) was an Indian aerospace engineer and statesman who served as the President of India from 2002 to 2007.

He was born and raised in a Muslim family in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu.
He studied Physics and Aerospace Engineering.
He worked primarily with DRDO and ISRO for several decades.
He contributed significantly to India's civilian space programme and military missile development.
He was popularly known as the "Missile Man of India".
He played an important role in the Pokhran-II nuclear tests in 1998.
He became the President of India in 2002.
He was widely known as the "People's President".
He received several awards, including India's highest civilian honour, the Bharat Ratna.
He died on 27 July 2015 while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong.
Career as a Scientist

After graduating from the Madras Institute of Technology in 1960, Kalam joined the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO).

He later worked with the Indian National Committee for Space Research and joined the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO).

At ISRO, Kalam became the Project Director of India's first Satellite Launch Vehicle, SLV-3, which successfully deployed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit in July 1980.

He also contributed to the development of the Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV) and India's missile development programmes.
